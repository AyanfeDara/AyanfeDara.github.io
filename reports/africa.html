Africa doubled its electricity supply, but not per person

Electricity generation in Africa grew 119% between 2000 and 2024, but population grew 83%, so electricity per person rose only 20% (534 to 639 kWh). Nigeria has the least electricity per person of seven major economies, and Ethiopia has overtaken it.

Tools
Power BI (Power Query, data modelling, DAX)
Data
Our World in Data, Energy dataset (Energy Institute Statistical Review, Ember), CC BY 4.0; 54 African countries, 2000 to 2024
Dashboard PDF ↗Files on GitHub ↗

Business question

Which African countries are growing electricity supply fastest, how clean is that growth, and where does Nigeria stand against its peers?

Findings

1. Supply more than doubled, but population growth absorbed most of it

Generation rose from 439 to 962 TWh (+119%), while population grew from 823 million to 1.5 billion (+83%). Electricity per person therefore rose only 20%, from 534 to 639 kWh. Five countries (South Africa, Egypt, Algeria, Morocco and Nigeria) produce 69% of the continent's electricity.

2. Countries took very different paths

Kenya's renewable share rose from 40% to 92%, led by geothermal and wind. Ethiopia's electricity stayed almost entirely hydro (100% renewable). South Africa still produces 84% of its electricity from fossil fuels.

3. Nigeria has the least electricity per person of the seven, and its renewable share fell

Nigeria generated 161 kWh per person in 2024, against 705 in Ghana and 3,867 in South Africa. Ethiopia, which started at 25 kWh in 2000, has overtaken Nigeria (191 kWh). Nigeria's renewable share fell from 38% to 25% because most of its new supply came from fossil fuels, which tripled from 9 to 28 TWh while renewables grew from 5.6 to 9.4 TWh.

Data and method

  • Cleaning (Power Query): kept 14 of the 130 columns; filtered to 2000 to 2024; kept only African countries with an inner join to a lookup table of 54 countries and their UN sub-regions.
  • Model: star schema, with a countries dimension (one) linked to the yearly energy fact table (many) on ISO code.
  • Measures (DAX): generation, renewable share, growth since 2000, and electricity per person calculated as total generation divided by total population, so large and small countries are weighted by their populations.

Limitations

  • Generation measures electricity produced, which is different from the share of people with access to electricity.
  • Uses the legacy release of the OWID energy dataset (GitHub, last updated 2026), which OWID no longer updates. Its electricity figures come from Ember and the Energy Institute; OWID's 2026 methodology change affects primary energy, not these electricity columns.
  • Data for the latest year is partly estimated by the source and may be revised.
